Zone Roads · bulletin 06 / 0.47

Route logs that do not fool your memory

A recorded landmark is more useful than a neat arrow if tomorrow you cannot remember where a warning came from.

Start with an anchor

Begin each note with level, in-game time and two landmarks. “North of the road” is not enough when a road splits and every rumour uses the same location name. Give your observation a short unique reference.

Keep three layers separate

A fact is something you witnessed. Testimony is what a named source said. An inference is your interpretation of the first two. Do not collapse them into a single sentence: after several retellings a theory can appear to be confirmed intelligence.

Mark what may have changed

After an emission, battle or long delay, flag old information for verification. Be especially cautious about enemy counts and supposedly clear paths. A static website does not know where squads or mutants are in your current save.

Plan two routes

Write down the primary approach, a point where you would turn back and a return route. An alternative need not be longer; it must remain understandable under worse conditions. Record hazards without invented survival percentages.

Make a readable report

Keep the essentials short: what you checked, when, how and what remains unknown. Traceability matters more than formatting. A note can be stored in the local Observation Log without automatically sending it to anyone.
